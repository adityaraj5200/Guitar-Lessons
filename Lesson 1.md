## Lesson 1 — Understanding your guitar 🎸

Your guitar has three major sections: the **headstock**, **neck**, and **body**.

The headstock is the end with the six tuning pegs. You've already used those while tuning.

The long section is the **neck**. On its front is the **fretboard**, where your left-hand fingers will press the strings.

The large hollow section is the **body**. The round opening is the **sound hole**. Your right hand will usually strum the strings around this area.

### The six strings

Your guitar has six strings. Hold it normally and look downward at them.

The **thickest string**, closest to your face, is string 6. The **thinnest**, closest to the floor, is string 1.

From thickest → thinnest:

| String |  Note | Description |
| -----: | :---: | ----------- |
|      6 | **E** | Thickest    |
|      5 | **A** |             |
|      4 | **D** |             |
|      3 | **G** |             |
|      2 | **B** |             |
|      1 | **E** | Thinnest    |

So memorize:

**E – A – D – G – B – E**

For now, don't worry about what E, A, D, etc. actually mean. We'll learn notes properly later.

---

## What exactly is a fret?

Look at your guitar's neck. You'll see many thin **metal strips** running across it.

Those metal strips are **fret wires**.

Here's one important terminology detail: when a guitarist says **"play the first fret,"** they usually mean **press the string in the space between the nut and the first metal fret wire**, rather than putting your finger directly on the metal wire.

Imagine the beginning of your guitar neck:

```text
HEADSTOCK

      NUT        fret wire     fret wire     fret wire
       │             │             │             │
───────│─────────────│─────────────│─────────────│────
       │   FRET 1    │   FRET 2    │   FRET 3    │
───────│─────────────│─────────────│─────────────│────
       │             │             │             │
```

So:

**Fret 1** = first space after the nut
**Fret 2** = next space
**Fret 3** = next space

And so on.

This distinction will become extremely important.

# Your first guitar exercise

Don't learn a chord yet.

We're first going to teach your left hand how pressing a string works.

Take the **1st string** — the thinnest string.

First, pluck it without pressing anything.

That's called playing the string **open**.

You can represent it as:

```text
1st string:

0
```

Here **0 = open string**.

Listen to its sound.

Now place the tip of your **left index finger** in fret 1 on that same string.

But there's a trick.

Don't press here:

```text
| FRET WIRE |
      👆
```

Instead, press **slightly behind the fret wire**:

```text
          👆
──────────●──│────────
             ↑
          fret wire
```

Press firmly enough and pluck the string again.

You should hear a **clear note**.

If you hear:

**BUZZZZZZ**

that's usually because you're not pressing firmly enough, you're too far from the fret wire, or part of your finger is interfering with the string.

---

## Now play your first sequence

On that same thinnest string, play:

```text
0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
```

That means:

**0:** don't press anything, pluck
**1:** press fret 1, pluck
**2:** press fret 2, pluck
**3:** press fret 3, pluck
**4:** press fret 4, pluck

Listen carefully.

You'll notice something interesting:

> **As you move toward higher frets, the sound becomes higher in pitch.**

That's one of the fundamental principles of guitar.

# Your left-hand fingers also have numbers

Guitar lessons commonly number your fretting fingers:

```text
Index  = 1
Middle = 2
Ring   = 3
Pinky  = 4
```

Your thumb isn't numbered because it normally supports the back of the neck.

Eventually, you'll encounter instructions like:

> "3rd string, 2nd fret, finger 1."

You'll know exactly what that means.

---

## Your first finger-training exercise

Now we're going to use all four fingers.

On the **1st string**:

```text
Fret 1 → index
Fret 2 → middle
Fret 3 → ring
Fret 4 → pinky
```

Play slowly:

```text
1 — 2 — 3 — 4
```

Don't try to be fast.

Your pinky will probably feel ridiculously weak and uncoordinated. That's completely expected; you're asking your hand to perform movements it hasn't previously trained for.

Then try the same thing on the **2nd string**.

That's enough for your first practice.

## Today's 15–20 minute session

Spend about 2 minutes making sure the guitar is still tuned. Spend 3 minutes memorizing **E A D G B E** and identifying strings 1–6. Then spend about 10 minutes playing **1-2-3-4** slowly across the first and second strings. Use the remaining few minutes simply plucking different strings and getting comfortable holding the instrument.

Don't worry about **chords, scales, strumming patterns, tabs, barre chords, or music theory yet**.

Once you can do this, our **Lesson 2** will introduce your first actual musical concept: **what a note is, why the strings are called E-A-D-G-B-E, and why pressing a fret changes the note.** Then we'll play your first chord.
