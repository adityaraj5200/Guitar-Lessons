# Lesson 2 — Notes, Frets & Your First Chord

You already know:

* Your guitar has **6 strings**.
* They are **E A D G B E** from thickest → thinnest.
* `0` means an open string.
* Frets are the spaces along the fretboard.
* Pressing farther up the fretboard makes the pitch higher.

Now we'll understand **why**.

## 1. What is a "note"?

When you pluck a guitar string, it vibrates. That vibration produces a sound with a particular **pitch**.

In Western music, we give pitches these seven basic letter names:

**A B C D E F G**

After G, the sequence starts again:

**A B C D E F G → A B C D E F G → ...**

So when we say your thickest string is an **E string**, we mean:

> When you pluck it without pressing any fret, it produces the note **E** when properly tuned.

That's why your tuner app showed letters such as E, A, D, etc.

---

## 2. Why are there sharps like F#?

There are actually **12 notes** before the pattern repeats at a higher pitch:

**A → A# → B → C → C# → D → D# → E → F → F# → G → G# → A**

Notice something unusual:

There is normally **no B# between B and C**, and **no E# between E and F** in this basic naming scheme.

For now, just remember:

> **One fret = one step to the next note in that 12-note sequence.**

This step is called a **semitone** or **half-step**.

---

# 3. Now your fretboard makes sense

Take your thickest string.

It's the **6th string**, and its open note is **E**.

Play it without pressing anything:

**Open → E**

Now press fret 1.

What's the note immediately after E?

**F**

Therefore:

```text
6th string

Open     Fret 1     Fret 2     Fret 3
  E   →     F    →     F#   →     G
```

Continue:

```text
0   = E
1   = F
2   = F#
3   = G
4   = G#
5   = A
6   = A#
7   = B
8   = C
9   = C#
10  = D
11  = D#
12  = E
```

Something cool happens at fret **12**.

You're back to **E**, but it sounds higher than the original E.

That's called an **octave**.

---

# 4. Try this physically

Pick your thickest string.

Play:

```text
0 → 1 → 2 → 3
```

And say aloud:

```text
E → F → F# → G
```

Don't worry about memorizing the entire fretboard. We're simply connecting what your **hand does** with what your **ear hears**.

Now try your **5th string**.

Its open note is **A**:

```text
0 → A
1 → A#
2 → B
3 → C
```

Notice again that B goes directly to C.

This is the beginning of understanding the fretboard rather than just memorizing finger positions.

---

# 5. What is a chord?

So far we've played **individual notes**:

```text
♪    ♪    ♪
E    F    G
```

A **chord** is multiple notes played together in a particular combination.

For example, an **E minor chord (Em)** contains the notes:

**E + G + B**

Your guitar lets us arrange those notes across all six strings and strum them together.

And that's going to be your first chord.

# 6. Your first chord — E minor (Em)

This is one of the easiest guitar chords.

Hold your guitar normally.

You need to press only **two strings**:

```text
6th E ─── 0 ───  ← open
5th A ─── 2 ───  ← press fret 2
4th D ─── 2 ───  ← press fret 2
3rd G ─── 0 ───  ← open
2nd B ─── 0 ───  ← open
1st e ─── 0 ───  ← open
```

So:

**`0 2 2 0 0 0`**

means E minor.

### Finger placement

Use:

**Middle finger (2)** → 5th string, 2nd fret
**Ring finger (3)** → 4th string, 2nd fret

Place both fingertips **close behind the second fret wire**.

Your other four strings remain untouched.

---

# 7. Don't strum immediately

First, test every string individually.

Hold the Em shape and slowly pluck:

```text
6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1
```

You want:

**Ding → Ding → Ding → Ding → Ding → Ding**

If one sounds like:

**buzz / thud / muted**

stop there and investigate.

Usually it's because you're not pressing firmly enough, your finger is too far from the fret wire, or one finger is accidentally touching the neighbouring string.

Once **all six strings ring clearly**, strum all six downward:

**↓**

You just played **E minor**. 🎸

---

# 8. Your first rhythm

Hold Em and perform four slow downward strums:

```text
     ↓       ↓       ↓       ↓

     1       2       3       4
```

Try to keep equal time between them:

**ONE... TWO... THREE... FOUR...**

Don't worry about up-strumming yet.

This introduces another fundamental concept: **rhythm**.

3. Hold Em and repeatedly strum **↓ ↓ ↓ ↓**, counting **1-2-3-4**.
